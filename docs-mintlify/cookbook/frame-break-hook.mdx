---
title: "Frame-break meta hook"
description: "Letterboxed setup. Actor pushes the white bars apart. Slam into full-bleed kinetic typography."
---

A 15-second 9:16 hook with a structural punch. Act 1 is the meme-header TikTok
format — white 9:16 canvas, 16:9 footage letterboxed in the middle, static
Helvetica meme-header above. At ~3.0s the actor visibly pushes the white bars
apart with both hands, the footage expands to full-bleed, and a "NO LIMITS"
typography slam lands on the beat. The structural break visualizes the hook copy
("no limits"), earns the kinetic-typography layer, and hides AI-slop behind the
meme-header in the first three seconds.

## Brief

> "Hook for a fitness app. 15s, 9:16. Open in the meme-header letterbox format with
> the header text 'EVERY GYM APP I'VE TRIED', actor in the letterboxed footage looks
> annoyed. At 3s the actor reaches out, pushes the white bars apart, the footage
> goes full-bleed, and 'NO LIMITS' slams across the screen. Then he demonstrates
> the app."

## Stack

- **Template:** [`social-hook`](https://github.com/alecs5am/ralphy/tree/main/templates/creator-lifestyle/social-hook) (`vibe-style`, creator-lifestyle) — the frame-break is a custom composition layer
- **Image anchor:** `google/gemini-3-pro-image-preview` (default since 2026-05-20) — persona master + scene anchors share the actor's identity across the frame-break, so multi-ref consistency matters more than label typography here
- **Video:** `kwaivgi/kling-v3.0-pro --audio` — two i2v clips (letterbox 4s + full-bleed 11s)
- **Refs needed:** none for the generic fitness-app archetype; gate fires if the user attaches a real app's UI
- **Runtime:** ~8 min cold-start
- **Cost:** ~$3.20 (two images + two Kling clips)

## Walkthrough

<Steps>

<Step title="Scaffold and lay out the two-act structure">
```bash
ralphy template use social-hook \
  --project no-limits-hook-001 \
  --name "No limits frame-break hook" \
  --brief "15s 9:16 frame-break hook for a generic fitness app: act 1 letterboxed meme-header, act 2 full-bleed slam"
```

The scenarist writes two scenes: scene-01 (`hook`, ~4s, letterboxed) and
scene-02 (`body`, ~11s, full-bleed). The frame-break is a composition transition
between them — render two clips at different effective aspects.
</Step>

```

```

<Step title="Generate the two Kling clips">
Clip 1 is 4s — actor reaches forward with both hands at the 3.0s mark as if
pushing something apart. Clip 2 is 11s — actor in full-bleed demonstrating the
app, with `--audio` carrying the voice.

```bash
ralphy generate video \
  --project no-limits-hook-001 \
  --slot scene-01-vid \
  --model kwaivgi/kling-v3.0-pro \
  --image workspace/projects/no-limits-hook-001/assets/image/scene-01-bg.png \
  --duration 4 \
  --prompt "Actor holds annoyed expression for ~2.5s, then visibly reaches BOTH hands forward toward the camera at 3.0s as if pushing two invisible vertical bars apart. Hands come from the center of frame and move outward toward the edges. NO speech, NO music, only room tone."

ralphy generate video \
  --project no-limits-hook-001 \
  --slot scene-02-vid \
  --model kwaivgi/kling-v3.0-pro \
  --image workspace/projects/no-limits-hook-001/assets/image/scene-02-bg.png \
  --duration 11 \
  --audio \
  --prompt "Actor in full-bleed 9:16, leans into camera holding the phone, energy shifts from annoyed to confident, walks the viewer through the app on his phone with subtle handheld camera, mouth animated. NO music. [VOICE: hyped English male, late-20s] Every gym app I've tried locks me into their plan. This one lets me build my own — sets, reps, rest, swap exercises mid-session, no paywall on the basics."
```
</Step>

<Step title="Compose the frame-break in Remotion">
The composition does the structural work the Kling clips cannot:

- 0.0s–3.0s: scene-01 16:9 clip scaled into a letterbox inside a white 9:16 canvas, static Helvetica meme-header "EVERY GYM APP I'VE TRIED" above the letterbox.
- 3.0s–4.0s: the white bars animate outward (transform-X) timed to the actor's hand push. Footage scales from 16:9 letterbox to full-bleed 9:16.
- 4.0s: hard cut to scene-02 full-bleed. "NO LIMITS" typography slam lands on the cut, drum-sting on the beat.
- 4.0s–15.0s: scene-02 plays full-frame with karaoke captions over the dialogue.

```bash
ralphy generate captions --project no-limits-hook-001 --audio workspace/projects/no-limits-hook-001/assets/video/scene-02-vid.mp4
ralphy render no-limits-hook-001
# → workspace/projects/no-limits-hook-001/render/final.mp4 (15.0s, 9:16)
```
</Step>

</Steps>

## Why this works

The meme-header TikTok format — white 9:16 canvas, 16:9 footage letterboxed,
static Helvetica meme-header above — is one of the most reliable scroll-stops
in 2026. The static header reads as commentary / Reddit-thread / news-cap copy,
and the letterboxed footage feels like a screenshot of something captured.
The whole shape hides AI-slop behind the meme aesthetic — even a flawed Kling
clip works inside a letterboxed window with bold header text dominating the
frame.

The frame-break is the second beat. Most kinetic-typography hooks slap text
over footage without earning it. The frame-break visualizes the hook copy ("no
limits") by literally breaking the letterbox limit — the actor pushes the white
bars apart, the footage expands, and the typography slam lands at the exact
moment the constraint dissolves. The pattern earns the kinetic-typography
layer narratively, instead of pasting it over the footage as an effect.

The composition does the structural work the model cannot. Kling does not
animate white bars sliding apart inside a clip — that's a Remotion transform
keyframed against the actor's hand motion. The model produces two clips with
the right hand gestures and the editor stitches them through the break.

Clip 1 stays motion-only with no `--audio` because the act-1 actor is silent —
the meme-header text is the voice of act 1. Clip 2 runs with `--audio` for the
on-camera dialogue. Both use the same persona master to lock identity through
the break.

## Variations

- **Vertical bar break.** Instead of pushing horizontal bars apart, the actor
  pulls a single vertical curtain open. Same template, same composition logic,
  different transform direction.
- **No-typography slam.** Drop the "NO LIMITS" slam. The frame-break alone is
  enough on a 8-10s shorter cut. Cheaper, faster, same structural hook.
- **Russian VO.** Drop `--audio` on clip 2. Generate motion-only, then ElevenLabs
  over the act-2 cut. The header text in act 1 stays Cyrillic.
- **Three-act extension.** A second frame-break at the 11s mark slams back to
  letterboxed for a final reveal scene. Adds ~$0.70 and ~3 min.
- **Different meme-header.** "POV: every gym app", "GUYS WHY", any short
  declarative header. Keep the typography static — animated headers compete
  with the frame-break for attention.

## Pitfalls

<Warning>
Do not try to animate the frame-break inside a single Kling clip. Kling
interpolates a "wide cinema cut" inside the 9:16 container and the result is
the actor zoomed in oddly while pretending to push bars apart. The break is a
Remotion transition between two clips, not a Kling effect.
</Warning>

<Warning>
The actor's hand gesture in clip 1 must hit the 3.0s mark precisely. If Kling
returns a clip where the push happens at 2.4s or 3.7s, the typography slam
loses sync with the gesture. Regenerate the clip once with explicit timing
("at exactly 3.0 seconds the actor pushes both hands outward"); if Kling still
drifts, accept the model timing and re-time the typography slam to match.
</Warning>

<Warning>
The meme-header text in act 1 must be static, not animated. Animated headers
compete visually with the frame-break beat and the audience reads it as two
unrelated effects rather than one coherent hook.
</Warning>
