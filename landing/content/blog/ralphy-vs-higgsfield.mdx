## What Higgsfield Supercomputer is, accurately

Supercomputer launched on May 14, 2026. It's Higgsfield's umbrella
product, an agentic stack that wraps their existing studios into one
"describe it and ship" loop. Inside it lives:

- **Cinema Studio 3.5** for narrative work — 3 camera bodies, 5 lens
  types, 5 focal lengths (including the new 75mm), 3 apertures, plus
  70+ motion presets (dolly, crane, FPV, crash zoom, bullet-time).
- **Marketing Studio** for UGC and ads — 25+ hook variations, the
  Virality Predictor (Hook Score, Hold Rate, brain-heatmap), and a
  Hermes-driven campaign builder.
- **Soul ID** for character consistency — train a digital double from
  your photos and the face holds across angles, lighting, and styles.
- **40+ built-in tools** orchestrated by the agent — scriptwriting,
  upscaling, audio mixing, and so on.
- **Multi-model out of the box** — Seedance 2.0, Kling 3.0, Veo 3,
  Sora 2, Wan 2.6, MiniMax Hailuo 02 on the video side; Nano Banana Pro,
  Seedream 5.0 Lite, Flux.2 Pro, GPT Image on the still side.
- **Swappable LLM driver** — the agent itself runs on your choice of
  GPT 5.5 Pro, Claude Sonnet, Claude Opus 4.6, or Gemini 3.1 Pro.

It's genuinely good work. Soul ID is the market leader for character
consistency. Cinema Studio's camera language has no real competitor on
the hosted side. The "one prompt to ad" loop is fast.

This article is not arguing that Higgsfield is a bad product. It's
arguing that for one specific kind of team, owning the pipeline yourself
is a better fit than renting theirs.

## The five things you actually own when you self-host

Hosted studios optimize for "type a brief, get a video." Ralphy
optimizes for the boring part that comes after: shipping ten reels a
week, regenerating scenes from last quarter, answering a CMO who wants
to know what you spent on hand-and-eyes regens in Q1.

- **Your keys, your rates.** Every model call goes direct to <Tag>OpenRouter</Tag>
  and <Tag>ElevenLabs</Tag> at list price. No platform markup, no
  credit-to-model lookup table.
- **A grep-able generation log.** Each project writes
  <Tag kind="neutral">generations.jsonl</Tag>: prompt, output URI, latency,
  cost, model id, append-only. Not a "history" tab in someone else's
  dashboard.
- **No expiring credits.** There's no Ralphy account, so there's no
  balance that resets at month-end and no 90-day timer ticking on
  top-ups you bought last quarter.
- **Reference fidelity in your repo.** <Tag kind="accent">ralphy ref</Tag>
  writes structured JSON for master shots, persona descriptors, and
  camera grammar. All committed. Still re-runnable next year, after the
  vendor has changed three model versions.
- **A deterministic render pass.** The final composition is <Tag>Remotion</Tag>,
  which is React plus headless Chromium. Caption timing, transition
  curves, music ducking: all TypeScript you can diff.

## What Higgsfield Supercomputer actually does better

Some things they ship today that Ralphy genuinely doesn't match.

A web UI a designer can click in. Ralphy is CLI-first; the assumed
driver is a coding agent. Higgsfield ships a clickable stack that
works without a terminal.

Soul ID character consistency. Training a digital double from a few
reference photos and getting consistent facial geometry across angles
and lighting is real, polished work. Ralphy's reference pipeline uses
checked-in master-shot images and `--ref` on each generate call, which
works but is less plug-and-play.

Cinema Studio's camera language. The combination of camera body, lens,
focal length, aperture, and 70+ named motion presets is more granular
than Ralphy's prompt templates. If your work depends on cinematic
camera grammar, Higgsfield gets you closer faster.

One-pass audio plus video. Higgsfield renders sync'd lipsync, ambient
beds, and music in a single agent call. Ralphy does this through Kling
`--audio` for English VO and an ElevenLabs overlay in the editor pass,
which is more steps.

Zero setup. No `bun`, no env vars, no `ralphy doctor`. If your team
doesn't write code, that gap is the whole conversation.

If those matter more than the audit log and the credit math, Higgsfield
is the right call. The point of writing this isn't to argue otherwise.

## What "AI-native" looks like in practice

The teams that get the most out of Ralphy share a few habits. None of
them are Ralphy-specific. They're just how AI-native teams operate in
general.

<Steps>
  <Step n="01" title="Treat prompts like code">
    Prompts live in git. Ralphy drops <Tag>prompts.json</Tag> and
    <Tag>STORYBOARD.md</Tag> into every project, both committed. Your
    prompt history sits next to your application code instead of in a
    SaaS dashboard somewhere.
  </Step>
  <Step n="02" title="Let an agent drive the CLI">
    The bundled agent reads `AGENTS.md`, picks the right playbook for
    the brief, then runs the matching `ralphy` verbs and writes the
    logs. The human reviews the diff and the mp4. Same loop as any
    pull request.
  </Step>
  <Step n="03" title="Retrofit, don't replace">
    Ralphy doesn't replace Figma, Notion, or Linear. It slots in
    between the brief and the render. The rest of your stack stays
    where it is, and migration happens one project at a time.
  </Step>
</Steps>

If that's the loop you're already in, or where you're heading by Q4,
you don't need a hosted studio. You need a CLI you can pin to a
specific commit and an agent that knows when to refuse to render.
Being able to grep your own prompt history in Slack is a nice bonus.

## Frequently asked questions

<FAQ>
  <FaqItem q="What's the actual difference between Ralphy and Higgsfield Supercomputer?" open>
    Ralphy is an open-source CLI you run locally, calling OpenRouter
    and ElevenLabs with your own keys. Higgsfield Supercomputer is a
    hosted agentic stack that owns the pipeline (studios, models,
    storage, the agent driving it) and bills you in credits. You're
    trading a polished web UI and Soul ID for an audit log, version
    control, and pass-through provider pricing.
  </FaqItem>
  <FaqItem q="Does Ralphy replace Higgsfield?">
    For AI-native teams already running CLI tools and coding agents,
    yes for most workflows. For teams whose creative process depends
    on Soul ID character consistency or the polished Cinema Studio
    camera grammar, no. Both products do real work; the question is
    which constraint set matches your team's habits.
  </FaqItem>
  <FaqItem q="How much does Higgsfield Supercomputer actually cost?">
    Three tiers, billed annually: Starter $15/mo with 200 credits,
    Plus $39/mo with 1,000 credits and unlimited access to certain
    image models, Ultra $99/mo with 3,000 credits and one unlimited
    video model of your choice. Top-up packs are roughly $5 per 100
    credits and expire after 90 days. Monthly credits don't roll over.
  </FaqItem>
  <FaqItem q="Why does credit expiry matter?">
    If you're on the Plus tier ($39/mo, 1,000 credits) and a campaign
    slips into the next month, you forfeit whatever's unused. If you
    buy a top-up pack to cover a launch and the launch gets delayed
    past 90 days, that pack is gone. Annual plans don't refund the
    difference. Ralphy doesn't have credits, so the question doesn't
    come up.
  </FaqItem>
  <FaqItem q="Can I use Ralphy and Higgsfield together?">
    Yes. Some teams use Higgsfield's Marketing Studio for
    hook-generation and Soul ID, then render the final cut through
    Ralphy + Remotion when they need an auditable build. They don't
    share state directly, but reference images and brand kits port
    over fine.
  </FaqItem>
  <FaqItem q="Does Ralphy have a free tier?">
    Ralphy itself is free and open source. The only cost is whatever
    OpenRouter and ElevenLabs charge for tokens, usually $0.40-$1.20
    per 6-scene 9:16 reel. There's no platform-side free tier because,
    well, there's no platform.
  </FaqItem>
  <FaqItem q="What models does Ralphy support that Higgsfield doesn't?">
    Both stacks ship the same frontier video models (Kling 3.0,
    Seedance 2.0, Veo 3, Sora 2, Wan 2.6, MiniMax Hailuo 02) and image
    models (gpt-image-2, nano-banana, Flux). The difference is the
    music layer: Ralphy uses ElevenLabs Music for genre-driven beds
    with no licensing chase. Higgsfield's audio is part of its
    single-pass video render and doesn't ship a standalone music
    primitive yet.
  </FaqItem>
  <FaqItem q="Can I bring my own brand kit?">
    Yes. Ralphy has first-class `brand`, `persona`, and `ref`
    resources. Drop in your logo SVGs, brand voice notes, and master
    shot images, and every project picks them up automatically. Soul
    ID is the equivalent on Higgsfield's side and is more polished
    today.
  </FaqItem>
  <FaqItem q="Does Ralphy support Russian or non-English voiceover?">
    Yes, through ElevenLabs Multilingual v2. Kling's `--audio` flag is
    canonical for English VO. For non-English we route to ElevenLabs
    and overlay in the editor stage, because Kling's accent slips on
    non-English inputs.
  </FaqItem>
  <FaqItem q="What about account bans and retroactive policy changes?">
    Documented Higgsfield concern: a wave of account closures during
    the 2025 holiday period and reports of previously approved content
    being retroactively blocked after policy revisions, with no refund
    on annual plans. Ralphy doesn't have a central account, so the
    failure mode doesn't apply. Your generations stay on disk; nobody
    can revoke them after the fact.
  </FaqItem>
  <FaqItem q="What ships in the box for templates?">
    43 templates across 5 segment-persona categories: B2B SaaS, DTC
    commerce, creator lifestyle, entertainment / viral, and cinematic
    narrative. 5 are full-stack production templates and 38 are prompt
    cookbooks with hooks, camera vocab, and worked examples.
  </FaqItem>
  <FaqItem q="Can I run Ralphy without an OpenAI account?">
    Yes. Ralphy never calls OpenAI directly. All LLM, image, and video
    routing goes through OpenRouter, which gives you access to OpenAI,
    Anthropic, Google, Black Forest Labs, Kling, Seedance, and others
    through one API key.
  </FaqItem>
  <FaqItem q="How do I migrate from Higgsfield to Ralphy?">
    There's no automated import; Higgsfield's storage is opaque. The
    migration looks like this: download your reference imagery, export
    whatever brand voice notes you wrote into the Marketing Studio,
    and recreate everything as `ralphy brand` / `ralphy ref` resources
    in your repo. After that, every new project runs through Ralphy.
    Most teams keep both going for a sprint and then deprecate the
    hosted account before the annual renewal hits.
  </FaqItem>
  <FaqItem q="What's the catch?">
    You have to install a CLI and manage two API keys. If that's a
    non-starter for your team, the rest of the trade-off doesn't
    matter. Ralphy is built for people who already run terminals.
  </FaqItem>
</FAQ>
